# Why do you want this role and what are your career expectations

---

## 🟢 Junior Level

### What the interviewer is really testing
Three things hide behind this question:
1. **Did you do your homework?** Candidates who know the product and stack stand out immediately.
2. **Is your motivation sustainable?** Someone chasing only a salary leaves in six months.
3. **Are expectations realistic?** A junior asking for a lead title or an unrealistic salary signals poor self-awareness.

### A formula for "Why this company / role"

```text
1. Something specific about the company   (product, engineering blog, domain, scale)
2. Something specific about the role      (stack, mentoring, type of problems)
3. How your skills / goals connect to both
```

### Sample junior answer
> "I read your engineering blog post on moving to Spring Boot 3 and virtual threads, and I liked that the team shares what went wrong as well as what worked. The role uses Java, PostgreSQL and Kafka, which are exactly the technologies I've been practising with my pet projects. I also saw you run a mentoring programme for juniors, which matters a lot to me at this stage. In return I bring a lot of energy and I'm a fast learner."

### Salary and five-year plan for a junior
- **Salary:** research the market range (Glassdoor, local salary surveys, recruiter info) and give a **range**, with the lower bound acceptable to you: "Based on my research for junior Java roles in this city, I'm looking at X to Y, but I'm open to discussing the whole package including learning opportunities."
- **Five years:** "In five years I want to be a confident mid-to-senior backend engineer who owns a service end to end and helps newer colleagues."

### Real-world analogy
It is like **dating vs. marriage**: the company wants to know you chose them for reasons beyond "they were available".

## 🟡 Middle Level

### Deeper example
> "In my current company I've reached the limit of what I can learn: it's a stable monolith with few releases. I want to work on a system where scale forces interesting trade-offs. Your platform handles a high event volume, and the job description mentions Kafka Streams and observability, both of which I've used only at a small scale. In the next two to three years I want to take on design ownership of a domain, and in five I'd like to be a senior engineer or tech lead, depending on whether I prefer the technical or the people side."

### Motivation: push vs. pull

| Type | Example | Interviewer reaction |
|---|---|---|
| Push (away) | "My manager is bad, the stack is old" | Negative, risk of repeating |
| Pull (toward) | "I want to work on high-load systems" | Positive, shows direction |
| Mixed (best) | "I've outgrown X, and Y is the next step" | Honest and constructive |

### Handling the salary question
1. **Delay** if possible: "I'd like to understand the scope first. What's the budget for this role?"
2. If pressed, give a **researched range** anchored to the market and your impact.
3. Consider the **total package**: bonus, equity, learning budget, remote policy, on-call load.
4. Never lie about your current salary; say you're focused on the market value for the role.

### Common mistakes
- Generic praise ("great company, great culture") with no specifics.
- Saying "money" as the first reason.
- Contradictory plans: "I want to be an architect" for a role that is purely maintenance.
- Not knowing what the company does.

## 🔴 Senior Level

At senior level, "why this role" is a **mutual evaluation**. You are assessing them too, and good interviewers like it.

### Lead-level answer
> "I'm choosing the next role on three criteria: problem complexity, the ability to influence technical direction, and the engineering culture. From your public talks I understand you are consolidating 60 microservices and fighting inconsistency in how teams do resilience and observability. That's a problem I've faced and solved partially: at my last company I created a platform starter and a paved-road approach. In five years I see myself as a principal engineer or engineering manager. I'm still deciding, and I'd value a company where both tracks are real and respected. Compensation matters, but I'm comfortable on that front if the scope is right."

### Questions to ask the interviewer (a high-value set)

```markdown
**Team and delivery**
- How does a change get from a pull request to production, and how long does it take?
- What does the on-call rotation look like, and how many pages per week?

**Technical health**
- How do you decide when to pay down technical debt?
- What was the last significant production incident and what changed afterwards?

**Growth and leadership**
- How are senior engineers evaluated, and is there a staff / principal track?
- What would success look like in the first 6 months?

**Honesty probe**
- What's the biggest challenge the team is facing that isn't in the job description?
```

### Negotiation at senior level
- Frame in terms of **impact and market**, not need.
- Have a **BATNA**: other offers or a good current job.
- Negotiate scope, title, equity, and remote flexibility, not only the base.
- Stay professional and collaborative: you will work with these people.

### Red flags
- A 5-year plan that is only "your job" or "CEO".
- No questions prepared: shows low interest.
- Being unable to say what the company does.
- Making salary the only theme.

## 🎯 Interview Cheat Sheet

### 30-second elevator pitch
"I'm drawn to this role because of <specific product/tech problem>, my experience in <X> lets me contribute early, and in five years I want to grow toward <track> by owning bigger problems."

### Must-know bullets
- Research the company: product, tech blog, recent news, stack, glassdoor reviews.
- Give **pull** reasons (what you move toward), not only push reasons.
- Provide a salary **range** based on research; talk about the total package.
- Have a believable but flexible 5-year plan.
- Prepare **5+ questions** for the interviewer; ask about delivery, on-call, growth, and challenges.

### Common follow-up questions
- "What do you know about us?"
- "What other companies are you interviewing with?"
- "What are your salary expectations?"
- "What would make you leave?"

### Red flags (things NOT to say)
- "I'll take whatever pays more."
- "I hate my current manager."
- "I have no questions."
- "I want your job in a year."
