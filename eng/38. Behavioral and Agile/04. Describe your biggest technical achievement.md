# Describe your biggest technical achievement

---

## 🟢 Junior Level

### What the interviewer is really testing
- Can you explain a technical topic to someone who wasn't there?
- Do you understand *why* you did what you did, not only *what*?
- Do you take pride in quality and results?

For juniors, the achievement does not need to be huge. A well-explained small win beats a vague big one.

### Choosing the story
Good junior achievements:
- Fixing a hard bug using a debugger, logs or a profiler.
- Improving a slow SQL query with an index.
- Building a complete pet project and deploying it.
- Raising test coverage or adding CI to a project.

### Sample junior answer (STAR)
> **S:** "On my capstone project, a Spring Boot bookstore API, the list-books endpoint took 4 seconds with 10,000 records.
> **T:** I wanted to understand and fix it before our demo.
> **A:** I enabled SQL logging and found the N+1 select problem: one query for books and one per book for the author. I replaced lazy loading with a `JOIN FETCH` query and added pagination via `Pageable`. I also added an index on `author_id`.
> **R:** The response time dropped to 120 ms, I wrote a short README note about it, and I now check SQL logs for every new JPA query."

### Small example

```java
// Before: N+1 queries (1 for books + 1 per author)
List<Book> books = bookRepository.findAll();

// After: one query, with pagination
@Query("select b from Book b join fetch b.author")
Page<Book> findAllWithAuthor(Pageable pageable);
```

### Real-world analogy
It's like a **portfolio piece** for a designer: you don't show everything, you show the one that proves your skill and explain the thinking behind it.

## 🟡 Middle Level

### Deeper example
A mid-level achievement must show **design decisions and trade-offs**, not only implementation.

> **S:** "Our nightly settlement batch grew from 1 hour to 6 hours and started overlapping with morning traffic.
> **T:** I was asked to bring it under 2 hours without new hardware.
> **A:** I profiled it with JFR and async-profiler: 60% of time was single-threaded row-by-row JPA updates. I considered three options: (1) scale vertically, (2) rewrite in plain JDBC batch, (3) partition and parallelise with Spring Batch. I chose (3) with chunk size 500 and partitioning by merchant ID, keeping JPA for reads and JDBC batch for writes. I verified correctness by running both old and new pipelines in parallel for a week and diffing the outputs.
> **R:** Runtime dropped from 6 h to 55 min, and no data discrepancies. The approach became the team's template for other batches."

### Trade-off table to cite

| Option | Pro | Con | Decision |
|---|---|---|---|
| Vertical scale | Zero code change | Costly, short-term | Rejected |
| Raw JDBC rewrite | Fastest | High risk, lost mapping | Partly (writes only) |
| Spring Batch partitioning | Scalable, restartable | Learning curve | Chosen |

### Common mistakes
- Describing the technology but not the **problem or the business value**.
- Taking credit for a team effort without clarifying your part.
- Missing numbers: "made it faster" vs "from 6 h to 55 min".
- Not mentioning what you verified or how you handled risk.

## 🔴 Senior Level

### Lead-level answer
Senior achievements are about **systemic impact**: architecture, reliability, cost, team productivity or business outcomes, and how you got other people to adopt your solution.

> **S:** "Our payments platform had five incidents in a quarter caused by cascading failures between 20 services; customers lost money and trust, and engineers burned out on on-call.
> **T:** As principal engineer I was asked to make the platform resilient within two quarters.
> **A:** I started with data: I classified 40 incidents and found that 60% came from missing timeouts and unbounded retries. Instead of a big redesign I created a shared Spring Boot starter with sane defaults (Resilience4j timeouts, circuit breakers, bulkheads, idempotency keys), and a 'resilience scorecard' dashboard. I ran game days with chaos injection with each team, and made the starter the easiest path rather than a mandate. I mentored two engineers to own it long term.
> **R:** Sev-1 incidents dropped from five to one per quarter, on-call pages by 55%, and 28 of 30 services adopted the starter voluntarily. It also became a reference in our hiring pitch."

```yaml
# The paved-road defaults: sensible resilience out of the box
resilience4j:
  circuitbreaker:
    configs:
      default:
        failureRateThreshold: 50
        waitDurationInOpenState: 10s
        slidingWindowSize: 20
  timelimiter:
    configs:
      default:
        timeoutDuration: 2s
```

### What to emphasise
- The **problem framing** with data (not assumptions).
- **Options considered** and why you rejected them.
- **Influence without authority** and adoption strategy.
- **Sustainability**: who owns it after you.
- **Business metrics**, not just technical.

### Red flags
- "I single-handedly" for a platform-scale change.
- Over-engineering stories with no measurable benefit.
- An achievement from 8 years ago with nothing recent.
- Cannot explain trade-offs when challenged.

## 🎯 Interview Cheat Sheet

### 30-second elevator pitch
"My biggest achievement was <problem> where <metric> was bad; I analysed it, evaluated options, implemented <solution>, and the result was <number>, which I then turned into a reusable approach for the team."

### Must-know bullets
- Pick a story with a clear **before/after metric**.
- Explain the **why** and the **alternatives**.
- State your **personal contribution** exactly.
- Be ready for a **deep technical dive** on any detail.
- Mention what you **learned** or would change.

### Common follow-up questions
- "What was the hardest part?"
- "What alternatives did you consider?"
- "What would you do differently now?"
- "How did you test it?"

### Red flags (things NOT to say)
- "I can't think of one."
- Claiming a team's work as yours.
- Metrics that sound invented or unverifiable.
- A story you cannot defend technically.
