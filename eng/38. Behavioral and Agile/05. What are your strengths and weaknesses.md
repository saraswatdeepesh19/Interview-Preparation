# What are your strengths and weaknesses

---

## 🟢 Junior Level

### What the interviewer is really testing
- **Self-awareness**: do you know yourself honestly?
- **Growth mindset**: do you work on gaps?
- **Relevance**: are your strengths useful for this job?

### Strengths: claim with evidence
A strength without proof is just a claim. Use the formula **Strength + Evidence + Value**.

> "I'm good at debugging systematically. In my internship I traced an intermittent failure to a race condition in a cache by adding structured logging and reproducing it with a test. That saved the team from rolling back a release."

Good strengths for a Java developer: debugging, attention to testing, fast learning, clear communication, reading other people's code, writing clean code.

### Weaknesses: real, minor and being fixed
Choose a genuine weakness that is **not critical** for the job, and show the **steps you are taking**.

> "I sometimes spend too long trying to solve a problem alone before asking for help. I've noticed that this slows me down, so now I use a 30-minute rule: if I'm stuck for 30 minutes, I write down what I tried and ask a teammate."

### What NOT to say
| Bad answer | Why |
|---|---|
| "I'm a perfectionist" | Cliché, reads as evasive |
| "I work too hard" | Fake weakness |
| "I have no weaknesses" | Zero self-awareness |
| "I'm bad at teamwork" | Critical flaw with no fix |

### Real-world analogy
It's like a **code review of yourself**: you list what is good, what needs refactoring, and the ticket you've opened to fix it.

## 🟡 Middle Level

### Deeper example
Mid-level strengths should be tied to **delivery and team impact**; weaknesses should show **an active improvement loop**.

> **Strength:** "I'm strong at turning vague problems into a concrete plan. Last year the business wanted 'faster search'. I measured the baseline, defined p95 < 300 ms as a goal, found the top three bottlenecks, and delivered them in order of impact within a sprint each."
>
> **Weakness:** "I tend to say yes to too many requests, which led to a sprint where I missed my own commitments. Since then I make trade-offs visible: when someone asks for something, I show my current list and ask what to deprioritise. In the last three sprints I met 100% of my commitments."

### Strength/weakness matrix

| Dimension | Strength example | Weakness example (with fix) |
|---|---|---|
| Technical | Strong SQL tuning | Less experience with frontend; pairing with a UI colleague monthly |
| Communication | Clear written design docs | Long meetings drain me; I prepare agendas and push async |
| Delivery | Reliable estimates | Over-polishing; I now time-box refactoring |
| Collaboration | Helpful reviewer | Over-explaining in reviews; I use checklists |

### Common mistakes
- Naming a weakness that is a **core requirement** ("I'm bad at writing tests" for a TDD team).
- Giving the weakness without the **improvement plan**.
- Strengths that are **generic** (hard-working, team player) with no proof.

## 🔴 Senior Level

### Lead-level answer
At senior level interviewers expect a nuanced profile: you know your **operating style**, how it helps and where it hurts the team, and how you compensate.

> **Strengths:** "I'm good at making complex trade-offs legible. I write short design docs and ADRs, so that a team can decide in a meeting instead of in three weeks of debate. I'm also good at building trust with non-technical stakeholders by translating technical risk into business risk.
>
> **Weakness:** "My instinct is to jump in and solve problems myself. As a tech lead that created a bottleneck: two engineers waited for me to review every decision, and I noticed their growth had plateaued. I changed my approach: I now define decision principles up front, delegate whole problems rather than tasks, and review designs rather than every PR. Within a quarter the team's lead time improved, and one of those engineers led a major migration."

### Self-awareness tools
- 360 feedback and peer reviews.
- Retrospectives focusing on your behaviour.
- A simple personal growth log.

```markdown
## Personal Growth Log (quarterly)
| Quarter | Observed gap | Experiment | Evidence it worked |
|---|---|---|---|
| Q1 | Bottleneck on design reviews | Delegate design ownership with ADR template | 3 ADRs authored by others |
| Q2 | Dominating discussions | Speak last in design meetings | More dissent surfaced early |
```

### Red flags
- Strengths all about being the "hero" who fixes everything.
- Weaknesses already solved years ago (looks rehearsed).
- Blaming organisation for weaknesses.
- No awareness of **how others experience you**.

## 🎯 Interview Cheat Sheet

### 30-second elevator pitch
"My strength is <skill>, which I demonstrated by <example with result>. My weakness is <real, non-critical gap>, and I'm working on it by <specific practice> which has already resulted in <small win>."

### Must-know bullets
- Strength = **claim + evidence + value to the team**.
- Weakness = **real + non-critical + active improvement**.
- Align strengths with the job description.
- Prepare 2-3 of each, so you can adjust.
- For senior roles, discuss how your style affects **other people**.

### Common follow-up questions
- "How do your colleagues see this weakness?"
- "What feedback did you get in your last review?"
- "Give an example where that weakness caused a problem."
- "What are you doing right now to improve it?"

### Red flags (things NOT to say)
- "I'm a perfectionist."
- "I don't have any."
- A weakness that disqualifies you ("I can't work in a team").
- Blaming others for your gaps.
