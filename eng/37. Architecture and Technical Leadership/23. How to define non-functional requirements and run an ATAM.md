# How to define non-functional requirements and run an ATAM

---

## 🟢 Junior Level

**Non-functional requirements (NFRs)** — also called quality attributes or "-ilities" — describe **how well** a system must work, not what it does: performance, availability, scalability, security, maintainability, observability, cost, compliance.

Vague NFRs are useless:
* ❌ "The system must be fast and highly available."
* ✅ "95% of search requests complete in < 300 ms and 99% in < 800 ms at 2,000 requests/s; monthly availability of checkout ≥ 99.95%."

### Make NFRs measurable with quality attribute scenarios
A scenario has six parts (SEI/Bass, Clements, Kazman):

| Part | Example |
| --- | --- |
| **Source** | A customer |
| **Stimulus** | Places an order |
| **Environment** | During Black Friday peak (10× normal traffic) |
| **Artifact** | Checkout service |
| **Response** | Order is confirmed |
| **Response measure** | p99 < 1 s, error rate < 0.1% |

---

## 🟡 Middle Level

### Common NFR categories with measurable forms

| Category | Measurable example |
| --- | --- |
| Performance | p95/p99 latency at a given throughput |
| Scalability | Handle 3× current peak by adding instances, with < 10% latency increase |
| Availability | 99.9% monthly per critical journey; RTO 1 h, RPO 5 min |
| Security | OWASP ASVS level 2; all PII encrypted at rest; MFA for admins |
| Maintainability | New developer deploys a change on day 2; lead time < 1 day |
| Observability | Every request traceable end to end; alerts on SLO burn |
| Cost | ≤ €0.002 infrastructure cost per order |
| Compliance | GDPR erasure within 30 days; audit log retained 10 years |

### Eliciting NFRs
* Ask "what happens to the business if…?" (5 minutes of checkout downtime, 1 hour of stale prices, a data leak).
* Use numbers from production (current traffic, growth forecasts) and from SLAs promised to customers.
* Prioritize: quality attributes conflict (security vs usability, consistency vs availability, cost vs redundancy). Rank the top 3–5 for the system.

### Turning NFRs into engineering artifacts
* SLOs and alerts (availability, latency).
* Load test scenarios with pass/fail thresholds.
* Fitness functions (ArchUnit rules, dependency/CVE gates).
* Security requirements in the Definition of Done.

## 🔴 Senior Level

### ATAM: Architecture Tradeoff Analysis Method
**ATAM** (from the Software Engineering Institute) is a structured evaluation of an architecture against its quality attribute goals, revealing **risks, sensitivity points and trade-offs** before they become expensive. A full ATAM involves stakeholders over a few days; teams often run a **lightweight version** in half a day to a day.

Main steps:
1. **Present the method** and the **business drivers** (goals, constraints, key quality attributes).
2. **Present the architecture** (C4 diagrams, key decisions, ADRs).
3. **Identify architectural approaches** used (caching, event-driven, CQRS, replication...).
4. **Build a utility tree**: quality attributes → refinements → concrete scenarios, each rated by business importance (H/M/L) and technical difficulty (H/M/L).
5. **Analyze** the high-priority scenarios against the architecture.
6. **Brainstorm and prioritize more scenarios** with a wider stakeholder group.
7. **Analyze again** for the new top scenarios.
8. **Present results**: risks, non-risks, sensitivity points, trade-off points, risk themes.

```text
Utility tree (excerpt)
Availability
  └ Region failure
      └ (H,H) "If eu-central-1 fails, checkout resumes in eu-west-1 within 15 min with < 1 min data loss"
Performance
  └ Peak load
      └ (H,M) "At 10× traffic, search p99 < 800 ms"
Modifiability
  └ New payment method
      └ (M,L) "Add a new payment provider in < 2 weeks without changing checkout core"
```

### Outputs and what they mean
* **Risk** — an architectural decision that may cause a quality attribute goal to fail ("single-region RDS: region failure RTO is hours").
* **Non-risk** — a decision that supports the goal ("stateless services allow horizontal scaling").
* **Sensitivity point** — a parameter that strongly affects a quality attribute ("checkout latency is sensitive to the pricing service timeout").
* **Trade-off point** — a decision affecting several attributes in opposite directions ("synchronous replication across regions improves RPO but adds 40 ms to every write").
* **Risk themes** — patterns across risks that point to systemic issues ("no tested DR anywhere").

### Making it practical
* Time-box (lightweight ATAM: 4–8 hours with architects, tech leads, product, ops/SRE, security).
* Feed results into ADRs, the backlog (with owners) and fitness functions (scenarios become load tests, chaos experiments, SLOs).
* Re-run for major changes or yearly; compare risk themes over time.
* Related lighter methods: architecture katas, risk storming, pre-mortems ("it's one year later and the system failed — why?").

## 🎯 Interview Cheat Sheet

**30-second pitch:** Non-functional requirements describe how well a system must behave and must be measurable — I write them as quality attribute scenarios with source, stimulus, environment, artifact, response and response measure, prioritize the top few with the business, and turn them into SLOs, load tests and fitness functions. ATAM evaluates an architecture against those scenarios using a prioritized utility tree and yields risks, non-risks, sensitivity points and trade-offs; in practice I run a lightweight, time-boxed version and feed the results into ADRs and the backlog.

**Must know:**
* Measurable NFRs vs vague statements.
* Six-part quality attribute scenarios.
* NFR categories and conflicts; prioritization.
* ATAM steps; utility tree with (importance, difficulty).
* Risks, non-risks, sensitivity points, trade-off points, risk themes.

**Common follow-ups:**
* How do you get product owners to define NFRs?
* Give an example of a trade-off point.
* How would you evaluate an architecture in one day?

**Red flags:** "high performance" without numbers; NFRs nobody tests; architecture reviews without business drivers.
